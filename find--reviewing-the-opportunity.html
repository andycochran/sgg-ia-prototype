Reviewing the Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO)

A Notice of Funding Opportunity, or NOFO, is the official announcement a U.S. government agency publishes when discretionary grants or cooperative agreements become available. NOFOs are attached to the opportunity listings you find when you search for grants. The listings contain the basic information about the opportunity, but the full details and requirements are in the NOFO.

Before you start applying, read through the NOFO several times. Doing this will help you understand if you can meet all the requirements.

Key Components of a NOFO

Most NOFOs contain similar sections, although the length and detail vary by program.

  • Program description: Contains the background and goals of the funding opportunity
  • Eligibility: Outlines who can apply. See Determining Eligibility for more details.
  • Funding details: List how much money is available and how many awards are expected
  • Application requirements: Detail what to submit, in what format, by when
  • Review information: Explains how your application will be evaluated and scored
  • Award details: Describe what happens after recipients are selected, including reporting requirements

What evaluation criteria tell you

Evaluation criteria are the specific standards an agency uses to judge your application. Read this section of the NOFO early, before you start drafting anything. Building your application around these criteria is the best approach.

Agencies look at things like how well you describe the problem you’re solving. They also look at your project plan, how you’ll measure success, and whether your budget makes sense. Most importantly, this section shows the agency whether your organization has the capacity to carry out the project.

Some NOFOs separate criteria into two groups:

  • Threshold criteria are pass/fail. You either meet them or you don't. For example, you might need to submit all required attachments or meet a basic eligibility rule. If you miss one, reviewers usually set your application aside before scoring even starts.
  • Scoring criteria are the weighted categories reviewers actually assign points to, once your application clears the threshold.

If you miss a threshold requirement, it could disqualify you, no matter how well the application scores elsewhere.
