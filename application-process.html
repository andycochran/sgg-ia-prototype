Grants Application Process

Learn About Grants

Before you search and apply for grants, it's a good idea to get an overview of the grants lifecycle. Learning about the grant process will make it easier for you and your team to navigate each step.

Determine Eligibility

There are a couple of things to consider when determining eligibility: basic eligibility and opportunity-specific eligibility.

Basic eligibility

The following types of organizations are generally eligible to apply for funding opportunities:

  • State, county, city, township, and special district government organizations along with Native American tribal government organizations
  • Independent school districts along with public and private institutions of higher education
  • Public and Indian housing authorities
  • Nonprofit entities, regardless of 501(c)(3) tax status
  • For-profit businesses and small businesses meeting SBA standards
  • Foreign applicants may qualify subject to legislation, agency policy, and U.S. tax requirements
  • Individuals have limited grant access and cannot receive personal financial aid

If you’re looking for a grant for personal financial assistance (small business, rent, home repairs, medical assistance), you’re not eligible for opportunities posted here. To find personal funding opportunities, please visit USA.gov.

Opportunity-specific eligibility

If you are a part of an organization that is eligible to apply, use our search tool to find potential opportunities. Once you find one, you should thoroughly review the Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO), which defines all the requirements for eligibility.

Review the Opportunity

As mentioned in the Determine eligibility section, once you find a potential grant, you should read through the Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO) before taking any other steps. It contains all requirements, including eligibility, page limits, and required attachments.

Get Ready to Apply

Before you start your application, you need to have a couple of accounts set up. Ideally, you’ll have a SAM.gov registration with an active unique entity identifier (UEI). You’ll also need a Grants.gov account. These accounts are required to submit an application.

Get instructions on how to complete the registration process.

Build Your Application

When it’s time to build your application, it’s a good idea to start prepping the materials before you jump straight into the application itself. Aside from the standard application forms and biographical information about your organization, most applications also require:

  • Needs statement: Identification of a problem and how your project will address it
  • Project description: Activities, target population, timeline, and staffing for the project
  • Goals and objectives: Outcomes you want to achieve and the steps you’ll take to get there
  • Evaluation plan: Measuring the success and effectiveness of the project
  • Summary and detailed budgets: Associated activities and costs

Learn more about how to build your application.

Submit Your Application

Before submitting, ask key stakeholders to review your application to make sure it meets all the requirements. Submit at least 24 to 48 hours before the deadline to leave time to fix any technical problems. Once submitted, check your account until the status shows "received by agency," and keep your tracking number for your records.
