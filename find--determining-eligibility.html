Determining Eligibility

Before beginning the application process, you should make sure you or your organization is eligible to apply for the grant. If you don't double check that you are legally eligible, you could waste a lot of time and money completing the application process for a grant you cannot receive.

For the full legal eligibility requirements, you need to carefully read the application instructions in each funding opportunity. The awarding agencies define eligibility in those instructions, and they may also summarize the eligibility in the synopsis or summary section.

Components of Eligibility

Read the NOFO’s eligibility section word by word. A common mistake: you see “nonprofit organizations” and assume it means any nonprofit. The opportunity might require a specific type of accreditation, or a minimum number of years in operation. Those details are easy to miss on a quick skim.

Eligibility covers 3 main areas: your organization, your project, and the funding itself.

Your Organization

What type of organization do you represent? Common categories include nonprofits, research institutions, government agencies, tribal organizations, and colleges or universities.

Some NOFOs narrow this further, down to specific categories:

  • Community or faith-based nonprofit
  • Museum, library, or arts group
  • Healthcare or human services nonprofit

Location matters too. Many grants are restricted to specific regions, communities, or countries.

Your Project

Eligibility is about more than just your organization type. It’s also about finding an opportunity that’s a good fit for your project goals and capacity.

Where will your project serve people? Some NOFOs limit funding to one city or county. Others cover multiple counties, a whole state, or the whole country.

What’s your project’s focus? Grants often support specific areas:

  • Education, healthcare, or the environment
  • Agriculture, housing, or energy
  • Research, arts and culture, or technology
  • Infrastructure or community development

Who does your work serve? Many NOFOs target specific groups, like youth, veterans, seniors, people with disabilities, immigrants, or low-income households. A NOFO might require you to serve a specific group or operate in a specified area. Make sure you meet these rules before you apply.

Funding Specifics

Finally, examine the funding details to make sure the grant fits your needs. Start with the cost-sharing rules, which tell you whether you need to provide matching funds, and how much. Next, think about what the grant will actually pay for, since grants commonly cover staff, equipment, a capital project, research, planning, training, or outreach. Make sure your plans line up with what’s allowed. Last, compare the funding amount to your organization’s annual budget. If you request far less than the minimum award, reviewers may see your project as too small for the program.
