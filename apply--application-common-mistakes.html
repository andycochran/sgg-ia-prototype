Common Mistakes to Avoid

Before you submit your application, learn from mistakes other applicants have made. These 10 mistakes come up again and again, according to grant reviewers and grant writing professionals.

Incomplete Forms

Don’t leave required fields blank. Reviewers often reject or delay applications simply because contact details, organization information, or project descriptions are missing. Check every section of your form, then check it again.

Missed Follow-Up

If a funder contacts you about missing information, respond right away. Most funders set a short window, often 2 to 3 weeks, for you to respond. If you miss it, your application may be closed as incomplete.

Too Much Jargon

Don’t assume the reviewer already understands your project. Avoid jargon and unexplained terms. If your proposal is hard to follow, even an experienced reviewer may struggle to tell what you’re trying to achieve. That hurts your chances.

Inconsistent Sections

Make sure your application flows as one connected story, not several unrelated pieces. If your project summary, budget, and project aims each describe a different idea, reviewers will notice. This often happens when organizations recycle a past application without fully updating it.

Budget Problems

Build a budget that holds up to scrutiny. Common budget mistakes include requesting items the grant doesn’t allow, using vague headings like “miscellaneous,” totals that don’t add up, and costs that don’t show good value for the funder’s money.

  • Requesting $50,000 when the minimum award is $250,000 will be seen as too small in scope.
  • If the grant requires a 20% match on a $200,000 award, you need to document $40,000 in non-federal funds.

Skipping the Significance

Explain clearly why your project matters. If you don’t show how your project fills a gap or solves a real problem, reviewers may pass on an otherwise solid proposal.

An Unrealistic Plan

Keep your project plan realistic. If your timeline or budget doesn’t match what you’re proposing to do, reviewers will question whether you can deliver.

Misalignment With the Funder’s Priorities

Make sure your project matches the funder’s mission and goals. A strong project can still lose if it doesn’t connect clearly to what the funder actually wants to support.

Not Asking for Feedback

If you’re rejected, ask the funder why. Most funders will explain what fell short, and many programs let you reapply once you’ve addressed the issues.

Missed Deadlines and Submission Errors

Submit early, not at the last minute. Technical problems, formatting errors, and last-minute mistakes are some of the most preventable reasons applications get rejected.

Formatting Mistakes

  • Exceeding page limits
  • Not using the correct font sizes or margin widths
