Grant Fraud & Scams

Every year, the federal government gives out hundreds of billions of dollars in grants. These grants go to universities, local governments, and organizations. Most of this money is used the right way. But misuse, deception, and abuse still happen. You can help protect the grant system’s integrity. Learn how to spot and report fraud.

What is Grant Fraud?

Grant fraud happens in many ways. Some of the most common examples include:

  • Charging personal expenses as business expenses against the grant
  • Charging for costs you never paid, or costs that don’t relate to the grant
  • Charging inflated labor costs or hours, including costs for employees, contractors, or consultants who don’t exist
  • Giving false information in grant applications or contract proposals
  • Billing more than one grant or contract for the same work
  • Faking test results or other data
  • Swapping approved materials for ones that weren’t approved
  • Lying about a project’s status to keep receiving government funds
  • Charging higher rates than those agreed to in the bid or contract
  • Pressuring government employees to award a grant or contract to a certain company, family member, or friend

Beyond wasting government resources, grant fraud carries serious consequences. According to the Department of Justice Office of Inspector General, these can include debarment from receiving future federal funding, civil lawsuits, and criminal prosecution.

Responsibilities of grant recipients

Organizations and individuals that receive federal grant funds are stewards of that money and are subject to oversight and audit. Recipients must:

  • Use grant funds only for their intended purpose
  • Keep records that justify all expenses
  • Understand that using grant funds for personal gain is considered theft, punishable under federal criminal and civil law

Prevent fraud as a grant recipient

Organizations can reduce their risk of fraud by:

  • Establish good accounting, internal controls, and recordkeeping systems
  • Start a compliance program that encourages your team to report suspected misconduct
  • Report suspected fraud directly to the awarding agency

What is a Grant Scam?

A grant scam is distinct from grant fraud committed by recipients. In a scam, someone falsely promises access to "free government money," often demanding a fee up front. In reality, the government doesn’t give grants to individuals for personal use, and there is never a cost to apply for a legitimate federal grant.

How to protect yourself from scams

Never pay to receive a grant. No legitimate agency, including HHS, will ever request payment, gift cards, or wire transfers in exchange for a grant.

Never share personal information with unsolicited contacts. Scammers may request your Social Security number, birth date, address, bank account details, login credentials, or identification documents. Legitimate agencies don’t request this information through social media or unsolicited messages.

Verify the website. Official federal websites use a .gov domain. Sites ending in .org, .com, or .us that claim to represent a federal agency are not legitimate.

Report suspected scams immediately. Don’t respond to any message requesting payment or personal information in exchange for a grant.

Common signs of a grant scam

Be wary if someone contacts you with any of the following claims:

  • "You're eligible to receive a government grant." The government doesn’t proactively contact individuals about grants they haven't applied for.
  • "Pay a small fee to receive your grant." Legitimate grant applications never ask for a fee.
  • "The Federal Bureau of Grant Awards has approved your request." This agency does not exist. Scammers often invent official-sounding names to appear credible.
  • "We're calling from Washington, D.C." Caller ID and email addresses can be manipulated to make it look like they’re coming from the federal government, regardless of the caller's actual location.
  • "This grant doesn't require an application." All federal grants require an application.
  • "You've won a grant through a random drawing." The federal government doesn’t award grants through raffles or lotteries.
  • "You can spend this grant however you choose." Federal grants are awarded for specific purposes like research, infrastructure, or community programs and NOT for personal expenses like tuition or consumer purchases.

How to Report Fraud or a Scam

Each federal agency has an Office of Inspector General (OIG) — an independent unit responsible for identifying and addressing waste, fraud, and abuse within that agency's programs. Find the OIG for each agency.

You can also report fraud or a scam by contacting:

  • HHS Fraud Hotline: 1-800-447-8477 (TTY: 1-800-377-4950)
  • Federal Trade Commission (FTC): File a complaint online, or call 1-877-382-4357 (TTY: 1-866-653-4261)
